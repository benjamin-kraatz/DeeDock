Window companion interactive mockup · constants from the companion spike

A small face sits in a tab that grows out of the focused window, tinted like its title bar. Hover wakes it. Click it: a text selection in the window becomes context right away. Without one, you get selection mode, where its eyes follow the pointer and you drag a region or click for the whole window. The tab stretches into a prompt bar, the answer card drops into the window, and you can copy, replace the selection, paste, or run a proposed command. Click another window to move it, drag a title bar to watch it stay attached, use the green light to maximize. Esc backs out.

What's real, what's simulated
  • Geometry from the lab (spikes/companion-lab/lab/CompanionModel.swift, CompanionViews.swift): tab 108×42, flare 11, corner 15; selecting width 330; prompt bar 470 + 2 × flare; face 56×32, compact 40×26, corner 0.38 × height; eyes 10×13 (compact 7.5×10), pupil 0.58 × eye width; pupil reach 2.6 / 2.2 scaled over 140 pt; card 470 wide, radius 16.
  • Motion from the lab: morph spring response 0.42 / damping 0.8, face spring 0.28 / 0.55, eye spring 0.3 / 0.7, hover scale 1.06, answered smile 1.4×, blink every 3.4 s for 0.12 s, falls asleep 1.6 s after the pointer leaves. Retract-and-emerge when focus moves is a proposal (spring 0.42 / 0.72).
  • Tracking modes use the spike's measurements at 360 pt/s: Predict trails 0 pt (moves from the same drag events as the window), SkyLight 806 one frame (6 pt), AX notifications arrive at about 10 Hz and 37 ms late (24 pt median). The readout shows the live offset while you drag.
  • Tints are the sampled title-bar colors: Petal #FFFFFF, Ghostty #363A42, Helium #222A3E (the strip under the tab, not the active tab's #3E455A).
  • Simulated: answers are canned. On-device timing follows the lab (first token about 1.5 s); Claude timing is a guess. Menu commands, paste and Replace only change this page. The API key never leaves the page.
  • Reduce Motion: springs become short eases, the face doesn't bounce or blink, and the eyes stop following the pointer.
